## 🔴 BREAKING / TOP STORIES

- **CHRIS BOSH SAYS HE "ALMOST DROPPED DEAD" IN JANUARY — AND HAS A WARNING FOR WEMBANYAMA.** In an August 3 Q&A, the Hall of Famer revealed he suffered **another pulmonary embolism in January 2026** and spent a week in hospital: *"I almost dropped dead pretty much and came back to life."* His message to Victor Wembanyama, who manages deep vein thrombosis: **"Take your medicine."** *(HoopsHype, ESPN, Hoops Rumors, Yahoo Sports)*
  - *Why it matters:* Bosh's career ended at 32 because his clots came back a **second** time. He's telling a 22-year-old generational talent, from direct experience, that the first episode isn't the story — the second one is. He also made clear retirement didn't solve it: this is a lifelong condition he manages daily. **No new setback for Wemby — this is advice, not news.**

- **THE JALEN DUREN IMPASSE NOW HAS NUMBERS, AND THEY'RE MILES APART.** ESPN's **Ramona Shelburne** confirmed the Duren–Detroit standoff has hit a full **"standstill."** Duren is seeking something closer to **5 years / $200M+**; the Pistons want to stay in the **mid-$30M per year** range — roughly $165M over five. Marc Stein says he could stay unsigned deep into August or September. *(ESPN, Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* That's a **$35–40M gap** — not a negotiating dance, a structural disagreement. And both sides have a case: Duren averaged **19.5 points and 10.5 rebounds** and made the All-Star team, but fell to **10.2 and 8.5 in the playoffs**. Detroit saw the playoff version. Duren's camp saw the All-Star. Either someone blinks or he takes the **~$9.5M qualifying offer** and becomes unrestricted in 2027 — at which point Detroit's matching rights vanish entirely.

- **THE MAVERICKS ARE NOW ACTIVELY SHOPPING KLAY THOMPSON — AND HE WANTS LOS ANGELES.** Multiple rival-team sources told **The Athletic's Christian Clark** on August 3 that Dallas is exploring trades for Thompson. Thompson would like to land in **LA**, where he lived as a child and still owns a home. Miami remains the most-mentioned suitor but has made **no trade progress** and is **hoping for a buyout**, where it could offer roughly **$8M** of its non-taxpayer mid-level. *(The Athletic via Hoops Rumors, SI, NBC Sports)*
  - *Why it matters:* This flips last week's story. On July 30 the reporting was that Thompson hadn't even raised a buyout and Dallas was passively open to offers. Dallas is now working the phones — and a **player preference** has entered the picture. Dallas carries **16 standard contracts** and must trim before the season, though it sits **$2.3M under the tax**, so there's no financial gun to its head.

- **BOBBY MARKS: FREE AGENCY IS "ESSENTIALLY OVER" — AND ONLY THREE TEAMS EVER HAD MONEY.** ESPN's salary-cap analyst published a fact-vs-fiction audit of the whole offseason on August 3. Headline findings: only **three teams** (Nets, Bulls, Lakers) had genuine cap room; only **35 free agents changed teams** (about 75% stayed put); **41 players were traded** — more than signed elsewhere; and Wembanyama **declined a 30% escalator** on his extension, leaving roughly **$50M** on the table. *(ESPN / Bobby Marks)*
  - *Why it matters:* This is the explanation for everything else in this brief. Duren, Watson, Kuminga, Mathurin and DeRozan aren't unsigned because nobody wants them — they're unsigned because almost nobody can **pay** them. The new CBA has pushed the league from free agency into extensions and trades. That's a structural shift, and it's the best explainer video available to you right now.

- **A FEDERAL COURT RULING HAS DRAFTED NBA PLAYERS WEIGHING A RETURN TO COLLEGE.** US District Judge **Charlotte Sweeney** in Denver issued an injunction on Friday, July 31, granting the **high school class of 2022** a fifth year of college eligibility for 2026-27 — the group the NCAA's new "five for five" rule left out. **Braden Smith** (Pacers 2nd-rounder, Purdue All-American) said August 3 he **won't** go back. **Dillon Mitchell** (Celtics 2nd-rounder) is reportedly considering a return to **St. John's**. The NCAA is appealing. *(NBC Sports, CBS Sports, Hoops Rumors, Front Office Sports)*
  - *Why it matters:* A player drafted by an NBA team choosing college instead is genuinely new. Also affected: **Tyler Nickel** (Knicks/Vanderbilt), **Richie Saunders** (Grizzlies/BYU), **Lajae Jones** (Warriors/Florida State). In the NIL era, a fifth college year can legitimately out-earn a two-way contract.

## 🏀 FREE AGENCY UPDATE

Day 35, and the market has run out of money — not interest. Bobby Marks' August 3 audit says it plainly: free agency is **essentially over**, and only the **Nets, Bulls and Lakers** ever had real cap room. Everyone else has been working with mid-level exceptions, bi-annuals and trade exceptions. Hoops Rumors' August 2 check-in adds the other half of the picture: **44 of the top 50 free agents have signed.** What's left is six names and a pile of camp deals.

And every one of those six is stuck on a *mechanic*, not a market:

- **Harden** — agreed with Cleveland; the holdup is **structure**, because how the money spreads determines Cleveland's second-apron room.
- **Kuminga** — his ask has fallen **below $20M/yr** (realistically **$15–18M**), but that's still above the $15M mid-level, so it **must** be a sign-and-trade, and **Atlanta holds the veto** and remains unimpressed.
- **Watson** — Denver is **over the second apron and literally cannot take players back** in a sign-and-trade until it clears room. That's the blocker, not the money.
- **Duren** — a $35–40M gap and a standstill.
- **Mathurin** — New Orleans is trying to dump **Jordan Hawkins'** contract to get under the first apron and free up mid-level money for an offer sheet.
- **DeRozan** — "expected to sign this week," suitor list growing.

**Market snapshot:** Sacramento surfaced in two stories at once — chasing a **Duren** sign-and-trade Detroit keeps refusing, and confirming **no buyout is planned for Zach LaVine**. Atlanta's Kuminga math got worse after trading for **Lu Dort**, which pushes a sign-and-trade nearer the tax. Dallas has 16 contracts and a Klay problem. Golden State quietly rebuilt its edges — Draymond, Horford, Porzingis, and now Gary Payton II back.

Peyton Watson in Denver. His qualifying offer is six and a half million dollars — I keep repeating that because it gets misreported constantly. Denver's contract offer is seventy million over five years, about fourteen million a year. Watson wants north of twenty-five million. And he is genuinely weighing taking that qualifying offer, playing one cheap season, and hitting unrestricted free agency in twenty twenty-seven.

But the actual blocker here isn't the money — it's the math. Milwaukee and Denver have been in active sign-and-trade discussions, and Denver is over the second apron, which means they physically cannot take players back in a sign-and-trade until they clear room first. So even when both teams want to do a deal, the deal is illegal until Denver dumps salary. That's your tell — if you see Denver make a salary dump move, the Watson trade is coming right behind it. Denver's asking price has also come down, from two first-round picks a week ago to roughly one first plus one notable player. Cleveland, Milwaukee, Atlanta and the Clippers are all still circling.

Bennedict Mathurin is quiet — no offer sheet has been filed. He's got an eight-point-seven-seven-million-dollar qualifying offer from the Clippers. The interesting mechanic is in New Orleans: the Pelicans are trying to move Jordan Hawkins' contract to get under the first apron, which would free up enough mid-level money to make Mathurin a real offer sheet, maybe starting around fifteen million. So watch the Jordan Hawkins move — that's the domino. Chicago and Milwaukee have been linked, Dallas has denied serious interest, and the Clippers want to keep him but can't move on anything while the Kawhi Leonard investigation is open. That investigation is now affecting decisions well beyond Kawhi himself.

And James Harden is still unsigned in Cleveland. Still expected back. Two or three years, and the last hurdle is the structure — how the money spreads out — because that sets how close Cleveland sits to the second apron.

Now, Klay Thompson, because his situation genuinely changed on Monday. And there's a new detail: Thompson wants Los Angeles. He lived there as a kid, he still owns a home there. Meanwhile Miami is still the team mentioned most often, because the Heat badly want shooting around Bam Adebayo and Giannis Antetokounmpo — but Miami has made no progress on a trade and is reportedly hoping for a buyout instead, at which point they could offer about eight million dollars from their mid-level. Thompson is on seventeen and a half million in the final year of his deal. He shot thirty-eight-point-three percent from three last season, which is still good, but he averaged career lows of eleven-point-seven points in twenty-one minutes. Dallas has sixteen standard contracts and has to trim the roster, though they're two-point-three million under the tax so there's no emergency. Dallas is shopping, Klay wants L.A., Miami wants a buyout. One of those three has to give.

Now the strangest story of the summer, and this one is a genuine first. A federal judge in Denver — U.S. District Judge Charlotte Sweeney — issued an injunction on Friday granting the high school class of twenty twenty-two a fifth year of college eligibility for next season. The background: the N.C.A.A. is bringing in what people call the five-for-five rule — five years to play five seasons, no redshirt years, transfers don't matter. That starts this fall. But it left out the class of twenty twenty-two, who'd just finished four years. The judge said no, they qualify too.

Here's why it's an N.B.A. story. At least five players who were drafted this June and haven't signed contracts yet are now eligible to go back to college instead. Braden Smith, the Purdue All-American drafted by Indiana, said Monday he's not considering it. But Dillon Mitchell, a Boston second-rounder, is reportedly weighing a return to St. John's.
