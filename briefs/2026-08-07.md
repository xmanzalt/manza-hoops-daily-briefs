## 🔴 BREAKING / TOP STORIES

- **DILLON BROOKS CASHES IN — 3 YEARS, $73M TO STAY IN PHOENIX THROUGH 2030.** The Suns locked up the 30-year-old off a career year: **20.2 PPG, 3.6 RPG, 1.8 APG, 34.4% from three in 56 games**. Phoenix acquired him last summer in the Kevin Durant package and went 45-37 with a playoff berth. The reaction online split hard down the middle — "$73 million to be annoying" versus "fully deserved." *(AP via Washington Post, NBA.com, Yahoo Sports)*
  - *Why it matters:* This is the first real money spent in over a week, and it's a statement about how Phoenix values the Durant trade. They gave up a top-5 player, got Brooks as a piece, and just committed to him through 2030. Either the Suns genuinely believe in this core or they're paying a premium to look competent.

- **THE PEYTON WATSON STORY JUST FLIPPED — HIS AGENTS ARE REPORTEDLY TRYING TO GET HIM OUT.** ESPN's Shams Charania reported Thursday that Watson's representatives now appear **focused on finding him a new team rather than negotiating with Denver.** That's a meaningful shift from "sign-and-trade interest exists" to "his camp wants out." Cleveland, Milwaukee, the Clippers and Atlanta are all offering "combinations of player packages, pick packages." The **Bucks remain reluctant to include a first-rounder** and would likely have to send valuable players instead — **Jaime Jaquez Jr. is the name floated** — but Milwaukee's first-apron hard cap complicates the salary math badly. Denver's offer sits at **$16–18M/yr**; Watson wants north of $25M; the **$6.5M qualifying offer with an implicit no-trade clause** is still on the table. *(Shams Charania/ESPN, Eric Nehm/The Athletic, Ryan Blackburn/Mile High Sports, via Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* Denver has said all along its intent is to keep him. If his agents are actively shopping him, this stops being a contract negotiation and becomes a standoff with a losing side. Watson's leverage is the QO — one year, $6.5M, no-trade protection, unrestricted in 2027.

- **ANTHONY DAVIS BECAME EXTENSION-ELIGIBLE THURSDAY — AND BOTH SIDES IMMEDIATELY AGREED TO WAIT.** August 6 was the third anniversary of Davis's last Lakers extension, making him eligible for up to **4 years / $274.9M** with Washington. Per The Athletic, no meaningful talks will happen until roughly the **20-game mark of the 2026-27 season**. The reason is blunt on both sides: Davis has never played a game for the Wizards and has appeared in just **62 total games over the past two seasons (20 in 2025-26)**. Washington wants to see him healthy; Davis wants to see the team's direction. He makes **$58.5M in 2026-27** with a **$62.8M player option for 2027-28**. *(The Athletic via Hoops Rumors, CBS Sports, RealGM)*
  - *Why it matters:* This is the most polite possible way of saying "neither of us is sure about this." Washington gave Trae Young $212M and traded for Davis — and now the biggest contract decision on the roster is being deferred until December. That's a franchise hedging.

- **THE KNICKS WILL OFFER KARL-ANTHONY TOWNS AN EXTENSION — BUT NOT THE MAX.** The Athletic's James L. Edwards III reports New York's front office intends to extend Towns but is **not expected to put the full 4-year, $272M max on the table.** Towns has been an All-Star in both Knicks seasons, playing **147 of 164 possible games** and averaging **22.2 PPG and 12.3 RPG** — and they just won the title. But with Brunson, Anunoby and Bridges already on long money, a full max makes the second apron nearly unavoidable. He holds a **2027-28 player option** he'd have to decline to extend in-season. *(The Athletic via Hoops Rumors, Yardbarker, ClutchPoints)*
  - *Why it matters:* A championship team telling its All-Star center "you're getting paid, just not what you're worth" is how title cores start cracking. Watch whether this becomes a trade conversation by midseason.

## 🏀 FREE AGENCY UPDATE

Day 38. **Four new contracts Thursday** — the busiest day since the start of August, even if none of them are headliners. Three are camp deals and one is an overseas departure. The six big names are all still unresolved.

**Where the big six stand this morning:**

1. **Harden (Cavs)** — Still agreed in principle, still unsigned. Reporting continues to describe Harden as being deliberately patient to help Cleveland's cap flexibility while they chase Watson.
2. **Watson (Nuggets RFA)** — **Changed materially Thursday.** Agents now reportedly working to place him elsewhere. Bucks won't send a 1st; Jaquez Jr. is the alternative. QO is $6.5M with an implicit no-trade clause.
3. **Duren (Pistons RFA)** — Unchanged. Little progress toward a deal, though "momentum and positive direction" toward a **25% max (5yr/$252M)** has been reported. He is genuinely considering the **~$9.5M QO** to reach unrestricted free agency in 2027. A sign-and-trade is described as **almost certainly not happening** — Detroit intends to keep him.
4. **Kuminga (UFA)** — **Minnesota has emerged as the latest suitor.** Lakers still at 2yr/$20M. Atlanta holds the sign-and-trade veto. Over a month unsigned.
5. **DeRozan (UFA)** — **Turns 37 today.** New wrinkle: the **Warriors have stayed in contact** since Sacramento waived him, but league sources **downplay the chance he accepts Golden State's veteran minimum** given interest from Washington, Miami, Denver, Cleveland and now Detroit.
6. **Mathurin (Clippers RFA)** — No new developments. Four teams inquired, zero offer sheets. **$8.77M QO** in hand.

**Extension watch — a name to add:** **Julian Strawther (Nuggets)** is now eligible for a rookie-scale extension. His minutes dropped last season after Denver added Tim Hardaway Jr. (who has since signed with Miami), so there's a real argument his role reopens in 2026-27. *(Hoops Rumors)*
