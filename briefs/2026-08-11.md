## 🔴 BREAKING / TOP STORIES

- **JOSH HART AGREES TO 4-YEAR, $81M EXTENSION WITH THE KNICKS.** Hart became extension-eligible on August 10 and quickly reached a deal worth a reported **$81 million over four years** — well below the **$131M maximum** he was eligible for. The Knicks' glue guy (12.0 PPG / 7.4 RPG / 4.8 APG / 50.8% shooting, 66 games last season) took a discount to keep the defending champions together. *(Yahoo Sports, Hoops Rumors, Rotoballer)*
  - *Why it matters:* This is the blueprint answer for whether the Knicks can survive the second apron. Hart following Brunson's discount template means New York keeps its core intact. The next test: Karl-Anthony Towns.

- **DUREN-PISTONS TALKS OFFICIALLY PAUSED — AND THE PISTONS NEVER OFFERED THE 30% MAX.** Despite Duren earning **first-team All-NBA honors** and his first All-Star nod during Detroit's 60-win season, the Pistons reportedly only put the **standard 25% maximum** on the table — not the **30% supermax** his All-NBA selection made him eligible for. Talks are paused and **not expected to resume until later in August, possibly September**. *(Hoops Rumors, HoopsWire, Yardbarker)*
  - *Why it matters:* The Pistons have all the leverage — Duren can't sign elsewhere as a restricted free agent — but refusing to acknowledge his All-NBA value risks a relationship problem with a 22-year-old franchise cornerstone.

- **HAWKS BACK OFF WATSON; CLIPPERS AND BUCKS NOW THE FRONTRUNNERS.** Atlanta has "backed off significantly" from Peyton Watson due to price resistance (Watson has been seeking $25M+/year; Pistons offered $14–18M). The **Clippers and Bucks** are now considered the most viable sign-and-trade suitors, with Milwaukee holding a **traded player exception** that could absorb Watson's salary — though the Bucks don't want to give up a first-round pick. *(Hoops Rumors, HoopsHype)*
  - *Why it matters:* Watson's market keeps shrinking. If Atlanta and Detroit are out, his only realistic landing spots require complex sign-and-trades involving teams with cap constraints of their own.

- **HALL OF FAME ENSHRINEMENT WEEKEND IS FOUR DAYS AWAY — AUG 14–15, SPRINGFIELD.** The Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame Class of 2026 will be enshrined August 15 in Springfield, with the Tip-Off Gala at Mohegan Sun on August 14. Class members: **Doc Rivers, Amar'e Stoudemire, Elena Delle Donne, Candace Parker, Chamique Holdsclaw, Mark Few, Joey Crawford (referee), Mike D'Antoni (contributor), and the 1996 U.S. Women's National Team.** *(HoopHall.com, NBA.com)*
  - *Why it matters:* Don Nelson's passing last weekend adds extra weight to this ceremony. Stoudemire played for D'Antoni's iconic Suns. Rivers coached 24 years in the league. It's a rich class.

## 🎙️ TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT

Good morning, it's Manza with Manza Hoops, and today is Tuesday, August 11, 2026. Let's get into everything that happened in the NBA yesterday, because it was a pretty significant day for one of the most important teams in the league.

The biggest story of the day is Josh Hart staying in New York. The Knicks' heart-and-soul guard became extension eligible on Monday — August tenth — and he and the team wasted no time getting a deal done. Hart agreed to a four-year extension worth a reported eighty-one million dollars. Now, to put that in perspective: he was eligible for up to a hundred and thirty-one million. He left fifty million on the table. That is the Jalen Brunson blueprint playing out in real time. Hart averaged twelve points, seven and a half rebounds, nearly five assists, and shot fifty-one percent last season — he is the glue of that championship team — and he chose to stay at a number that keeps the defending champs financially flexible. The next question for New York is whether Karl-Anthony Towns does the same thing.

Now let's talk about the restricted free agent market, which is stuck in amber. Three big updates on that front.

First: Jalen Duren and the Detroit Pistons have officially paused talks, and here is the detail that has everyone talking — the Pistons reportedly never even offered Duren the thirty-percent maximum that his All-NBA selection made him eligible for. They put the standard twenty-five percent max on the table. Duren had his best season — first All-Star, third-team All-NBA, the backbone of a sixty-win team — and Detroit responded with a lowball. Talks are expected to resume in late August, possibly September. The Pistons have all the leverage because he cannot sign elsewhere as a restricted free agent. But refusing to pay your twenty-two-year-old franchise centerpiece his market value is a relationship risk.

Second: Atlanta is out of the Peyton Watson sweepstakes. The Hawks backed off significantly because Watson is seeking around twenty-five million a year and Atlanta was not willing to meet that number. The teams now considered most realistic for a sign-and-trade are the Los Angeles Clippers and the Milwaukee Bucks. Milwaukee has a traded player exception that could work here, though they don't want to give up a first-round pick. Watson was Denver's most tradeable asset for months and yet here we are in August with nothing done.

Third: Bennedict Mathurin. Detroit is officially out. The Pistons had checked in on the Clippers restricted free agent but have moved on. Any deal for Mathurin would require a sign-and-trade, and with the Kawhi Leonard investigation still freezing all Clippers cap activity, that remains almost impossible to execute right now.

On Jonathan Kuminga: the Lakers remain his primary suitor, but Kuminga is reportedly unhappy with what Los Angeles has offered. The Cavaliers are also exploring a sign-and-trade. Minnesota can only offer him the veteran's minimum. The twenty-three-year-old averaged over twelve and a half points and five and a half rebounds last season. This market has been a disappointment for him.

On Klay Thompson: Dallas is not budging. The Mavericks are actively exploring a trade for the four-time champion, but they are firmly resisting a buyout. Miami remains the most frequently mentioned suitor. But Dallas is well under the luxury tax line, has zero financial pressure, and will not eat that contract for free. Until a trade materializes — or Klay becomes desperate enough to take a significant pay cut — he stays a Maverick.

A quick programming note before we look ahead: Jaylen Brown has been opening up about his trade to Philadelphia. He called it a "crazy transition" in a new ESPN piece. Brown is now joining Joel Embiid, Tyrese Maxey, LeBron James, and V.J. Edgecombe in what is arguably the most loaded roster in the Eastern Conference. The Celtics got Paul George and two first-round picks in return. The Eastern Conference is genuinely terrifying next season.

Looking ahead: we are four days away from Hall of Fame Enshrinement Weekend. The ceremony is Saturday, August fifteenth, in Springfield, Massachusetts, with the Tip-Off Gala on Friday the fourteenth at Mohegan Sun. The class includes Doc Rivers, Amar'e Stoudemire, Elena Delle Donne, Candace Parker, Chamique Holdsclaw, head coach Mark Few, longtime referee Joey Crawford, contributor Mike D'Antoni, and the nineteen-ninety-six United States Women's National Team. It is an incredible class — and coming just days after the passing of Don Nelson, this ceremony will carry extra emotional weight. D'Antoni and Nelson basically co-invented the modern NBA. Having D'Antoni get enshrined this week feels especially meaningful.

We are also waiting for updates out of Melbourne, where Ben Simmons is going through Australia's national team mini-camp this week — full-contact, zero restrictions. If he looks good on film, that changes his free-agent market quickly.
