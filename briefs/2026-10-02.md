## 🔴 BREAKING / TOP STORIES

- **DUREN SIGNS: FIVE YEARS, $200M, FULLY GUARANTEED.** Jalen Duren accepted Detroit's offer Thursday night, shortly before the 11:59 p.m. ET qualifying-offer deadline. No options, no incentives, $40M a year. Agent Chafie Fields confirmed it to Shams Charania. *(ESPN; AP; Detroit News; Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* The longest standoff of the offseason is over, and the East's top seed has its All-NBA center locked in through 2031.
- **HOW IT GOT DONE.** Detroit's offers went $175M, then $190M, then $200M, and the weight-related language was removed from the final version. The alternative was the one-year, $9.6M qualifying offer and unrestricted free agency in 2027. *(Hoops Rumors; The Athletic via Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* Duren got the clause out but not more money. He missed the first three days of camp.
- **RAPTORS EXTEND JAMAL SHEAD: THREE YEARS, $24M.** Runs through 2029-30, starts in 2027-28, third year is a team option. Shams reported it; the deal is finalized. *(ESPN; Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* Toronto locks in its backup point guard cheaply next to Kawhi. Shead led all NBA reserves with 354 assists last season.
- **LAS VEGAS EXPANSION: THREE FINALISTS, DECISION SOON.** Shams reported (Sept 29, still the live story Oct 1) the NBA will pick a winning bid "in the very near future" at $12–13B or more, franchise plus arena. *(Shams on X; TSN; theScore)*
  - *Why it matters:* That price would be the biggest in sports history. Target is the 2028-29 season.

What's up everybody, welcome back to Manza Hoops. It's Friday, October second, twenty twenty-six, and this is your daily NBA rundown, everything that happened in the league on Thursday.

We start in Detroit, because the standoff is over. Jalen Duren has signed. With less than an hour left before his qualifying-offer deadline, Duren agreed to a five-year, two-hundred-million-dollar contract with the Pistons. It is fully guaranteed, forty million dollars a year, with no options and no incentives. His agent, Chafie Fields, confirmed the deal to Shams Charania.

Here is how it got there. Detroit's offer started at one hundred seventy-five million, went to one hundred ninety, and then to two hundred million. The earlier versions had weight-related language in them, which Duren's side hated. The final version took that out. So Duren did not get more money by waiting, but he did get the clause removed and every dollar guaranteed. His other choice was a one-year deal for nine point six million dollars and unrestricted free agency next summer. He took the security.

Remember who this is. Duren is twenty-two. He averaged nineteen and a half points and ten and a half rebounds last season, shot sixty-five percent, and made All-NBA Third Team. But in the playoffs he dropped to about ten points a game, and the top-seeded Pistons went out in the second round. He missed the first three days of camp, so now the question is how fast he gets back up to speed.

Second story. The Toronto Raptors have extended Jamal Shead. It is a three-year, twenty-four-million-dollar extension that runs through twenty twenty-nine thirty, with a team option on the last year. Shead played all eighty-two games last season and led every bench player in the league with three hundred fifty-four assists. He called the offer, quote, honestly, above fair. Good value for Toronto next to Kawhi Leonard.

Third, expansion. Shams Charania reports the NBA is expected to choose the owner of the Las Vegas expansion team in the very near future, and the price is at least twelve to thirteen billion dollars for the team and a new arena. There are three finalist groups. One is Nancy Walton Laurie and Bill Laurie. One is Steve Apostolopoulos and Marc Lasry, the former Bucks co-owner. And one is Bill Foley, who owns the Vegas Golden Knights, together with Jerry Colangelo. No winner yet. The target is the twenty twenty-eight twenty-nine season.

On to injuries. In Toronto, Kawhi Leonard is still reconditioning. He only did drills in Thursday's scrimmage and he is unlikely to play in Saturday's preseason opener. Several other Raptors sat out as a precaution, including Kyle Anderson with a knee bruise. The good news there is Jakob Poeltl, who looked sharp after a back injury limited him to forty-six games last year.

In New York, Josh Hart will play through a finger issue, and coach Mike Brown says the starters will be eased in during preseason after a short summer as champions. In Orlando, Anthony Black has a minor ankle sprain and Noah Penda has a hamstring issue, and both are out to start camp. No new updates on Brandon Ingram, Bradley Beal, or Kristaps Porziņģis.

Around the camps. Stephen Curry is expected to start the Warriors' preseason opener on Sunday. JJ Redick plans to stagger Luka Dončić and Austin Reaves for the Lakers, and Bronny James is fighting for rotation minutes. Jaylen Brown says he is learning a whole new system in Philadelphia. And in Dallas, Zaccharie Risacher is settling in next to Cooper Flagg.

Transactions, confirmed. Besides Duren and Shead, the Suns picked up next season's options on Ryan Dunn and Khaman Maluach. The Mavericks guaranteed Moussa Cisse's salary. The Bulls signed guard Isaiah Stevens to a camp deal and waived Peter Suder. The Jazz waived Jaelen House, the Trail Blazers waived Hyunjung Lee, and the Rockets waived two-way player Rafael Castro. The day before, the Nuggets signed Ryan Nembhard to a two-way contract. And off the court, Rogers now owns one hundred percent of the Raptors' parent company. No trades.

Now the rumor mill, and these are reports, not done deals. Extension talks between Karl-Anthony Towns and the Knicks have stalled. Towns is eligible for about two hundred seventy-six million dollars over four years. Reports put the Knicks closer to two hundred million. That is a gap of roughly seventy-six million dollars, and nobody expects it to close before opening night. He stays eligible through next June.

Landry Shamet says two years on non-guaranteed contracts gave him perspective, and his new deal will not make him comfortable.
