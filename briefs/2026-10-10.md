## 🔴 BREAKING / TOP STORIES

- **KNICKS PAUSE ALL CONTRACT EXTENSION TALKS.** The champions announced Friday they "will not be negotiating contract extensions until later in the season." That freezes Karl-Anthony Towns and Josh Hart (Miles McBride and Tyler Kolek are also eligible). *(ESPN — Shams Charania, via ABC7; Newsday — Steve Popper, via Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* Towns is eligible for about $276M over four years; the Knicks' last offer was reportedly about $200M over four. He said this week the sides were far apart. Owner James Dolan wants to stay under the second apron. Towns has a $61M player option for 2027/28, so he can reach free agency next summer.
- **ALPEREN ŞENGÜN: TRIPLE-DOUBLE IN 22 MINUTES IN MACAO.** Rockets 135, Mavericks 117 in the first NBA China Game. Şengün: 16 points, 10 rebounds, 10 assists. Houston led by as many as 28 and shot 18-of-36 from three. *(ESPN box score; Eurohoops)*
  - *Why it matters:* First look at Houston with Fred VanVleet back — 17 points and 7 assists in his first game since May 2025 (torn ACL). **Kyrie Irving did not play**; he is reportedly expected back Sunday in game two.
- **NBA PUBLICLY PUSHES BACK ON BRUCE BROWN.** The league posted a statement saying claims the second apron is hurting "lower" and "middle" class players are "untrue," citing nearly $1.3B in added salaries and raises of about 30% "for all classes of players." *(NBA PR, via Hoops Rumors)*
  - *Why it matters:* Brown, in Knicks camp on a non-guaranteed deal, told SNY's Ian Begley the new CBA is good for max guys but "the middle guys, it kind of sucks for us." A league rebuttal to one player's quote is unusual.
- **PISTONS ASKED ABOUT KEVIN DURANT AND KAWHI LEONARD THIS SUMMER (REPORT).** ESPN's Vincent Goodwill reports Detroit inquired on both and was ready to make Austin Reaves a big offer. None of it happened: Houston would not engage on Durant, Leonard reportedly was not interested, and the Lakers gave Reaves the max. *(ESPN buzz)*
  - *Why it matters:* It explains the Duren standoff ending at five years, $200M — Detroit looked for a star first.
- **WARRIORS WORTH $14.5 BILLION.** Sportico's annual valuations: Warriors No. 1 for a sixth straight year (up 28%), Knicks $12.8B, Lakers $12.5B. League average $6.64B, up 21%. *(Sportico, via Hoops Rumors)*
